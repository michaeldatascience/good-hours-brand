A handheld red light is a small device you hold or strap on for a set number of minutes. The Good Hours Recovery Light is the palm-size version we sell: an aluminum body, 12 four-core LEDs, four wavelengths, two modes, and a timer. This note is about how to use a device like that at the end of the day. It is not a list of results. We are not going to tell you what the light will change. We are going to tell you how a session goes.

Start with what is in the box. You should have the light, eye protection goggles, a storage box, a charging dock, a USB-C cable, a power adapter, and a manual. Charge the light before the first night you use it. The battery is 4,000 mAh. Charging is USB-C, on the dock. There is no app to install and no account to make. If a button on a website is asking you to subscribe, you are not on the device. The device itself has one button.

Learn the two modes before you sit down, so you are not reading a label in the dark. Mode 1 is 660 and 850 nm. Mode 2 is 630 and 830 nm. Together those are the four wavelengths: 630, 660, 830 and 850 nm. Output at the LED surface is 100 mW/cm². The timer choices are 5, 10, 15 or 20 minutes. When the time is up, the light turns itself off. That is the whole interface. You do not pair it with a phone.

Pick a seat where you can stay still. A sofa is the usual spot. A chair in a garage gym works after a workout. A hotel chair works on the road. You do not need a blackout room. A normal evening lamp is enough. A dimmer room makes the red glow easy to see, and it makes the 15 minutes feel like a stop, not another task.

Put the goggles on before the LEDs are pointed at your face. Do not look straight into the light. Rest the device on the area you picked, or hold it a few inches away. The hand straps are there so you can let the arm relax. If you are holding it the whole time, your shoulder may be the thing that feels tired, which misses the point of an evening routine.

A first session of 10 minutes is a solid start. A lot of people who keep the habit land between 10 and 15 minutes. Use 20 if you already know you like the longer sit. The light feels gently warm. It is not a heater, and it should not feel hot. If your skin is unhappy, stop and take it off.

Skip the light on broken skin. Skip it if you are sensitive to light. If you are pregnant, if you have an implanted device, or if you have a health condition, ask your doctor before the first session. It is not for children. Those rules are also on the product page and the safety page. They are short on purpose.

You can pair the light with the rest of the night without making it complicated. After training, some people use a small massage gun on large muscles first, then sit with the light. After a desk day, 10 minutes on the shoulders or the legs is enough. Before bed, the light can come first and a warm eye mask can come after. On a trip, the light fits in a carry-on. Check airline battery rules before you fly. That line is still open on our product page, so do not guess.

Keep the routine boring, in the good sense. Same chair. Same timer. Same goggles in the same drawer. The device does not need a subscription to keep working tomorrow. You bought it once. Wipe the aluminum with a dry cloth, set it back on the dock, and leave it. If you are packing, the storage box is the right place, not a loose pocket with keys.

Weight and the exact outer size are still being confirmed from a sample. Check the product page before you plan a very tight pocket. The stand-in photo on the draft site is not the final picture. The shot note under it says what the real photo should be.

If you want the short version, here it is. Charge the light. Pick a mode. Wear the goggles. Set 10 minutes. Sit down. Let it turn off. That is the routine. The specs, the precautions, and the kit it pairs with are on the Recovery Light page if you want the numbers in a table.
