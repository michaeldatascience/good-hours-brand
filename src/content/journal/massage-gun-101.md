A massage gun is a handheld device with a motor that moves a head back and forth. The large ones are easy to spot in a gym bag. The Good Hours Percussion Mini is the smaller version: 1.4 lb (620 g), a brushless motor, three speeds, and five heads. One of those heads can warm or cool. This note is about where to put it, where to keep it away, and how long to stay. It is for temporary relief of minor muscle aches. That is the job. It is not a longer list than that.

The speeds run from 2,800 to 3,200 rpm. Amplitude is 0.31 in (8 mm). The battery is 2,400 mAh, about 3 to 5 hours of use, and the device shuts itself off at 15 minutes. Noise and the final word on the charge port are still being confirmed from a sample. The working guess on charging is USB-C. There is no app. You are not signing up for anything when you turn it on.

Start slower than your ego wants. Low speed, a standard head, and a large muscle. Calves are a common first spot after a run or a long drive. Quads and glutes are next. The muscles of the upper back can be fine if you stay off the spine itself. Shoulders can be fine if you stay on the muscle and off the joint. Glide. Do not park the head in one hole and lean your body weight into it.

Here is the do-not list, and it is not optional. Do not use the gun on the front of the neck. Do not use it on the head. Do not use it on the spine. Do not use it on joints. Do not use it on bruises. Do not use it on broken skin. If a spot makes you want to pull away, move off it. A time limit per spot is still open in the manual. The working note is TBD: 2 minutes, and we will confirm that from the sample before launch. Until then, shorter is the right call.

The warm and cool head is the one people ask about. Warm covers 95 to 113 °F (35 to 45 °C). Cool covers 50 to 68 °F (10 to 20 °C). Use that head on the same large muscles, not on a joint and not on the neck. Warmth is for the end of the day, when you want to unwind. It is not a reason to go longer than the timer. If the head feels hotter than the range above, stop and let it cool. We would rather you end early than argue with a number.

A simple after-training block is about 15 minutes, and the gun is only part of it. Sit down first. You are finished training. Use the gun for a few minutes per area, then put it down. If you also have a red light, that is the second half: goggles on, 10 minutes, timer in charge. The Recovery Kit is those two devices together. You do not need both to use the gun well.

After a desk day, the gun is optional. A lot of people only need the light. If your legs feel tight from the chair, a short pass on the calves is enough. Before bed, many people skip the gun and use warmth from an eye mask instead. Percussion is a wake-up feeling for some and a wind-down for others. Try it in the early evening before you decide it belongs at 10 p.m.

Travel is where the small size matters. At 1.4 lb it can live next to the light in a carry-on. The travel case is sized for both. Hotel carpet is a fine place to sit. Use the same speeds you use at home. Do not invent a new routine because the room is new. Check airline rules for the battery before you fly. We have not printed a final travel card yet.

Who should pause before the first session: anyone pregnant, and anyone with a health condition. Ask your doctor first. The gun is not for children. If you are not sure whether a spot is muscle or joint, skip the spot. Joints are on the do-not list even when they feel loud.

The short version: large muscles, low speed, keep it moving, stay off the neck, head, spine, and joints, stop at the timer. Temporary relief of minor muscle aches. Then you are done. The spec table on the Percussion Mini page has the rest of the numbers.
