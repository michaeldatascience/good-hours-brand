If you sit for work, the end of the day can feel like the day never ended. The inbox is still open. The shoulders are up by your ears. A 20-minute wind-down is a way to close the door without turning it into another project. This is the version we use when we talk about the Wind-down Kit: a handheld red light, then a heated eye mask. You can do it with one device. You can do a shorter version. The point is a stop time.

You do not need a spare room. A sofa is enough. A bed is enough if you can stay awake for the first half. A hotel room works the same way, which matters if your week is mostly other people's furniture. Dim the overhead light. A warm lamp is plenty. Put the phone face down, not across the room in a dramatic way, just face down. You are not in a test.

Minutes 0 to 10 are the Recovery Light, if you have it. Charge should already be done. You do not want to spend the first three minutes hunting for a cable. Goggles on before the LEDs face you. Mode 1 is 660 and 850 nm. Mode 2 is 630 and 830 nm. Either one is fine for this routine. Set 10 minutes. Rest the light on the shoulders, the upper back away from the spine, or the legs, or hold it a few inches off the area. Then sit. The timer ends the session. You do not get to negotiate with it.

What this half is not: it is not a stretch program, and it is not a second shift of email with a gadget in your other hand. If you are reading, you are not in the routine. The light feels gently warm. It is not a heater. Ten minutes is the desk-day length we put on the routines page. Twenty is the whole wind-down, not the light alone.

Minutes 10 to 20 are the Heated Eye Mask. Lie down. Fit the mask so the pressure feels even, not pinched. The first nights, use the lowest warmth. The mask is thermostat controlled, with air pressure and vibration. The exact temperature, the battery size, and the auto-off time are still being confirmed from a sample, so do not invent a number if someone asks you. It should feel warm, not hot. If it feels hot, take it off. That is a complete and correct session.

Do not walk around in the mask. Do not drive. This is a lying-down step on purpose. Ten minutes is enough. If the mask stops on its own, you are done. If you fall asleep, that is your evening, not a result we are claiming. We do not promise sleep. We promise a device that gets warm and a block of time with a stop.

If you do not own the mask yet, end at minute 10. If you do not own the light, start lying down with the mask and keep the whole thing to about 10 minutes until you know how the warmth feels. The 20-minute version is both, in that order: light, then mask. The kit price is lower than the two prices added together. There is still no app and no subscription in either half.

A few house rules sit under the routine. The light stays off broken skin, and you wear the goggles. The mask is not for children. If you are pregnant, if you have an implanted device, or if you have a health condition, ask your doctor before you make this a nightly habit. None of this replaces a person with a license. It is a gadget routine for general wellness and relaxation. The footer of the site says the same thing in fewer words.

Desk-specific notes, because the chair is the real opponent. You do not have to "fix" eight hours in twenty minutes. You have to stop. If your legs feel tight, a short pass with a small massage gun on the calves can sit between the two halves, but then keep an eye on the clock so the whole thing does not become 40 minutes. The gun stays on large muscles. It stays off the front of the neck, the head, the spine, and joints. A lot of desk routines are better without it. Warmth and a timer are the core.

When you are finished, dock the light and set the mask where you will find it tomorrow. That is the whole close. Twenty minutes, two devices, one order. If you want the steps without the essay, the routines page has the short list, and the product pages have the specs.
